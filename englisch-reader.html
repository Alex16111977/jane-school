Chapter 1: The Old Phone A2
Maya finds something unexpected in a second-hand shop

Maya loved Saturdays. On Saturdays, she didn't have to go to school. She could wander around the city and look at things in shop windows. Her favourite place was a small second-hand shop on Green Street. The shop had old books, strange jewellery, and things that people didn't need anymore.

On this Saturday, Maya was looking at a box of old electronics. There were broken headphones and cables good for nothing. Then she saw it — a small phone. It was old, but not too old. The screen was cracked in one corner, but the phone looked like it could still work.

"How much is this?" Maya asked the old man behind the counter.

He blinked at her through his thick glasses. "One euro," he said. "Maybe two. It probably doesn't work."

"I'll take it for one," said Maya. She liked a good bargain.

At home, she plugged in the phone. After ten minutes, the screen lit up. The phone was working. No password. Someone had forgotten to lock it.

There were apps, old photos, and — messages. Many unread messages. The last one was sent two years ago. Maya looked at the name of the person who had sent it.

Kai.

She didn't know any Kai. But she was very curious.

She pressed the message.

Chapter 2: The Messages A2
Maya reads and discovers something strange

The message from Kai was short: "I found it. I know where it is. Don't tell anyone — especially not him. I'll explain everything tomorrow. — K"

Maya read it three times. Found what? Who is "him"? The message was mysterious. And it was sent two years ago. Nobody had replied.

She scrolled through the other messages. Most were from someone called Lena. Lena and the phone's owner were clearly good friends. They talked about school, teachers, a dog called Biscuit, and a boy they didn't like. Normal stuff.

But between the normal messages, there were others from Kai that stood out. Kai kept talking about something he had found — something important. He used words like "secret", "proof", and "dangerous".

Dangerous? Maya felt a small shiver go down her back. She looked at the photos. Most were selfies of a girl with short dark hair. But there was one photo that was different — an old, grey building with wooden boards on the windows. Under it someone had written: "This is where it all started."

Maya zoomed in. She could see a street sign in the corner. Blackwood Road. She knew that road — only fifteen minutes from her house.

She should probably hand it in to the police. But she didn't want to. Not yet.

She wanted to know more about Kai.

Chapter 3: Searching Online A2/B1
Maya tries to find information about Kai

That evening, Maya opened her laptop and typed the name "Kai" into a social media search. There were thousands of results. She needed more information.

She went back to the phone and looked carefully at the photos. There was a boy with curly red hair and freckles, always smiling. In one photo, he was wearing a school hoodie with the word "Riverside" on it.

Riverside School. That was in her town. Maya typed "Kai Riverside School" into the search bar and scrolled through the results.

Then she found something. A small news article. The headline read: "Local Student Kai Fletcher Missing — Family Asks for Help."

Maya's heart sank. Missing. Kai was missing.

She read carefully. Kai Fletcher, 15 years old, had disappeared in October two years ago. He had left home for school but never arrived. The police had searched but found nothing. The case was still open.

Under the article, there were comments. One caught Maya's attention: "Kai was my best friend. If anyone has information, contact me — Leo." He had left an email address.

Maya stared at the screen. This wasn't just an interesting mystery anymore. This was a real person. A real boy who had gone missing.

And she was holding his phone.

Chapter 4: Writing to Leo B1
Maya decides to contact Kai's best friend

Maya hesitated for a long time before she opened her email. What could she even say? Hi, I found a phone that might belong to your missing friend. It sounded strange, even in her head.

But she wrote the email anyway — short and honest. She explained she had bought an old phone and found messages that mentioned someone named Kai. She said she wasn't sure if it was the same Kai, but wanted to check. She signed off with her name and phone number.

She pressed send, closed her laptop, and tried to do her homework. She couldn't concentrate. She kept thinking about the building on Blackwood Road. What had Kai found there?

The next morning, she had a reply. It was from Leo.

His message was long. He said yes — Kai Fletcher had owned a phone like that. He said Kai had bought it second-hand himself, which Maya found ironic. He said that before Kai disappeared, Kai had been obsessed with something — something in an old building near the river. He wouldn't say exactly what, but kept hinting it was important. Dangerous, even.

At the end, Leo wrote: "Could we meet? I've been looking for this phone for two years."

Maya wrote back immediately: "Yes. When and where?"

They agreed to meet at the café on Park Street the next afternoon. Maya put the phone carefully in her bag. She had a feeling that tomorrow would be significant.

Chapter 5: Meeting Leo B1
Two strangers talk about a boy they both care about

Leo was already in the café when Maya arrived. He was tall and serious-looking, with dark circles under his eyes. But he stood up when he saw her and gave her a nervous smile.

She sat down and placed the phone on the table between them. Leo looked at it for a long moment without touching it. Then he picked it up slowly, like it might break.

"That's his," he said quietly. "I remember this crack in the corner. He dropped it once when we were cycling." He looked up. "Where did you find it?"

Maya explained. Leo listened without interrupting. When she finished, he was quiet.

"He didn't just disappear, you know," Leo said finally. "I think he left on purpose. I think he was scared."

"Scared of what?" Maya asked.

Leo leaned forward. "Before he disappeared, he kept talking about someone he called 'The Manager'. He said this person was doing something wrong — stealing money that belonged to the community centre on Blackwood Road. And Kai had found evidence."

"The building in the photo," said Maya.

Leo stared at her. "You saw it?"

"Yes. And the message: I found it. Don't tell anyone."

Leo was silent. Then: "That was probably the last message he ever sent. Next morning, he was gone." He swallowed. "What if he was in danger? What if something happened because he knew too much?"

Maya thought. "Or — what if he left voluntarily to protect himself? And maybe… he's still out there?"

Leo looked at her with something like hope in his eyes. "Do you really think so?"

Chapter 6: Blackwood Road B1
Maya and Leo visit the old building

It was Leo's idea to visit the community centre on Blackwood Road. Maya said yes before he finished asking. To be honest, she had been thinking about it since she first saw the photo.

They went on a Sunday morning. The building looked exactly like the photo — grey walls, boards on the windows, a sign so faded you couldn't read it anymore. There was a chain on the front door, but the side door was open.

"We probably shouldn't go in," said Leo, already stepping inside.

Maya followed. Inside it was dark and smelled of damp and old wood. Broken chairs were stacked in a corner. Old posters covered the walls — community dinners, youth clubs, art exhibitions.

"This was once a really nice place," said Leo quietly. "My mum used to bring me here."

At the back they found a small office with a door slightly ajar. Inside were old filing cabinets. Most were empty. But in one drawer, Maya found a folder full of papers.

She flicked through them — financial documents. She wasn't an expert, but even she could see the numbers didn't add up. Thousands of euros listed as "management fees" with no explanation.

"Is this what Kai found?" she asked.

Leo stared at the papers. "It must be. This proves someone was stealing from the community centre. That's why it closed." He lowered his voice. "That's what Kai knew."

Maya photographed every page. "Let's keep these safe," she said. "And then — I think we need to talk to Kai directly."

Leo looked at her. "You still think he's alive?"

"I do," said Maya. "And I think he's been waiting for someone to find this."

Chapter 7: Finding Kai B1
A message from the past finally gets an answer

That night, Maya had an idea. She opened Kai's phone and went to the contact list. There was a number saved as "Mum". She hesitated. Then she sent a message:

"Hi. This isn't Kai. My name is Maya. I found this phone. I'm trying to find Kai. Please don't be scared — I just want to help."

She put down the phone and went to bed. She didn't expect a reply that night.

But in the morning, there were three messages from Kai's mother. The first two were suspicious and anxious — who was Maya, how had she got the phone? But the third said something different:

"Kai is safe. He has been living with his aunt in Edinburgh for two years. He fled because he was scared. He thinks about what happened every day. If you really want to help — talk to him yourself. Here is his new number."

Maya read it four times. Then she sent a screenshot to Leo.

His reply came in seconds: "HE'S ALIVE. Oh my god. Maya."

She called the number. It rang twice. Then a boy's voice answered — a little cautious, a little tired, but real.

"Hello?"

"Hi," said Maya. "My name is Maya. I found your phone in a second-hand shop." She paused. "And I think I found your proof."

Silence. Then: "The documents in the old office?"

"Yes. They're safe. I photographed everything."

Another silence. And then she heard something she didn't expect. Kai — this boy she had never met, this name on an old phone — was crying.

Chapter 8: The Truth Comes Out B1
The end — and a new beginning

A month later, Maya sat in the same café where she had first met Leo. This time, there was a third person at the table — a boy with curly red hair and freckles, who looked a bit thinner and older than in the photos, but had the same energy in his eyes.

Kai Fletcher had come back to his hometown for the first time in two years.

The documents Maya had found were now with a journalist who worked for the local newspaper. The journalist had investigated, found more evidence, and published an article that shook the whole community. "The Manager" — a man named Derek Hollis — had stolen over eighty thousand euros from the community centre. The police had opened a formal investigation.

"You could have just gone to the police yourself," Maya told Kai. "Two years ago."

"I know," said Kai. He stirred his coffee. "But I was fifteen. Derek Hollis was a powerful man here. I thought nobody would believe me." He paused. "I thought the easiest thing was to just… disappear."

"And was it easy?" Leo asked quietly.

Kai shook his head. "No. It was the hardest two years of my life. I missed everyone." He looked at Leo. "I missed you most."

Leo looked at the table. Maya pretended to be very interested in her hot chocolate.

Later, walking home alone, Maya thought about everything that had happened because of one decision — her decision to spend one euro on a broken phone. She thought about how a small act of curiosity could change everything.

Her phone buzzed. A message from Kai: "Thank you for not giving up. And for not giving the phone back to the shop."

She smiled and wrote back: "It was only one euro. Best purchase I ever made."

— The End —
